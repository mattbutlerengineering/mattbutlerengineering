# Big ideas — 2026-10-11

First entry. `docs/ideas/` didn't exist before this run, so nothing here repeats or builds on an earlier week.

**The market in one line:** in the last 30 days, guest-side AI agents went mainstream, and the big platforms responded in two ways. Resy banned one ([CNN, 2026-09-23](https://krdo.com/money/cnn-business-consumer/2026/09/23/now-ai-is-trying-to-gobble-up-dinner-reservations/)). DoorDash launched DashOS, which pulls marketplace data into reservations ([Technobezz, 2026-09-30](https://www.technobezz.com/news/doordash-launches-dashos-restaurant-platform)). Toast launched labor agents ([NRN, 2026-10-07](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/toast-unveils-an-array-of-new-ai-agents-for-restaurants)). A small independent operator gets the agent traffic without the controls, and is pushed toward someone else's walled garden. Most ideas below aim at that gap.

**What the repo can build on today:** availability, holds and the booking engine (`services/reservations/src/services/availability.ts`, `hold.ts`, `book-slot.ts`, `slot-rules.ts`). Deposits run on a Stripe state machine (`deposit-state-machine.ts`, `routes/stripe-webhook.ts`). The guest CRM covers recognition, reliability, risk, lapse detection and win-back (`guest-recognition.ts`, `guest-reliability.ts`, `guest-risk.ts`, `lapse-detector.ts`, `win-back.ts`). There are SSE live updates (`sse-connection-manager.ts`), a waitlist (`waitlist.ts`) and a pre-shift briefing (`briefing.ts`). On the frontend there is the booking widget (`apps/hospitality/src/components/booking-widget/`), the floor plan editor, and the Timeline and Waitlist pages (`apps/hospitality/src/pages/`). Rialto provides `TapeChart`, `Timeline` and `ChatPanel` (`packages/rialto/src/components/`). An agent with reservation tools already exists (`services/agent/src/routes/gen-agent-tools.ts`: `check_availability` and friends). So does an MCP server package (`packages/mcp-server/`), but today it only carries infra tools.

## The five

### 1. Agent Front Door

**Pitch:** let your guests' AI assistants book you directly, under rules you set, and stop treating them as bots to ban.

**Who hurts today:** a 40-seat wine bar gets a sold-out Friday. A guest's personal assistant polls the booking page every few seconds. To the venue it looks like a scalper, so the venue blocks it, and loses a regular who was only trying to delegate. When Resy faced exactly this, its only tool was a ban: a VC's agent "Instinct" was making about 200 requests per hour, and Resy says it "does not currently permit unapproved third-party bots or agents" ([CNN via KRDO](https://krdo.com/money/cnn-business-consumer/2026/09/23/now-ai-is-trying-to-gobble-up-dinner-reservations/); [Eater via Sequenzy](https://www.sequenzy.com/email-examples/brands/eater/emails/why-the-tech-bros-are-getting-banned-from-resy)).

**Why now:**

- ChatGPT added restaurant booking through OpenTable, Resy and Yelp on 2026-08-10 ([releases.sh](https://releases.sh/release/rel_KXqfnr8PTtwmVrlJiDWYG-restaurant-reservations-findable-in-chat-with-opentable-resy-yelp)).
- Google AI Mode books restaurants agentically in the UK through partners including SevenRooms and ResDiary ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/googles-ai-mode-now-books-uk-restaurants-meet-the-8-partner-platforms/)).
- Resy exposes a hosted MCP endpoint ([mcpservers.org](https://mcpservers.org/remote-mcp-servers/resy)).
- Stripe's Shared Payment Token gives a merchant scoped, revocable access to a guest's payment method for an agent-initiated purchase ([Stellagent](https://stellagent.ai/insights/stripe-shared-payment-token-spt); secondary source, check against Stripe docs).

Every piece an agent needs to book and guarantee a table now exists, except a venue-side counterparty.

**Boldest version:** every venue on the platform runs its own negotiating agent. A guest agent asks for "a quiet 4-top around 8 on Friday, flexible ±45 min, will pre-pay". The venue agent answers with offers tuned to yield, the guest's reliability and the kitchen's pacing. Examples: "7:15 at table 12 with a $20 deposit, or 8:30 with none". The two agents settle with a scoped payment token. Agent traffic stops being a cost and becomes the highest-quality channel, because agents always show up with intent, constraints and a payment method. The venue publishes an agent policy (rate limits, deposit rules, how bulk holds are handled) the way a site publishes `robots.txt`.

**First wedge (2-4 weeks):** a public, per-venue MCP server with tools `search_availability`, `place_hold` and `confirm_with_deposit`. These wrap the existing public routes (`services/reservations/src/routes/public-availability.ts`, `holds.ts`, `public-deposits.ts`) and copy the tool shapes already in `services/agent/src/routes/gen-agent-tools.ts`. Add an `agentPolicy` block to venue settings (max queries per minute per agent, deposit required for agent bookings, hold TTL), enforced next to `middleware/public-rate-limit.ts`. Demo: Claude Desktop, configured as a guest, books a table at the demo venue end to end, deposit included. The new booking shows up live on the operator's Timeline over SSE.

**What would kill it:** discovery. Guest agents go where the big aggregators already are (OpenTable, Resy, Google partners), and an independent venue's endpoint is invisible unless a registry or `llms.txt`-style convention emerges. The defensible core is the policy and negotiation layer, not the endpoint.

**Charter fit:** theme 1 (close the guest-facing loop) and the agent platform. No non-goal is challenged.

**Scores:** ambition 5 · feasibility 4 · wow 5

### 2. Tonight Autopilot

**Pitch:** a co-manager that watches the whole room live and tells you the next three moves before you need them.

**Who hurts today:** at 7:40 on a Saturday a 6-top is running 25 minutes long. A party of 4 on the waitlist is about to walk out, and the host is juggling three things at the stand. A copilot would see it immediately: table 9 will free up 20 minutes early if the dessert-stage 2-top moves to the bar, so the waitlist party can be seated at 7:55. Today the host works that out in their head, or doesn't.

**Why now:** Toast launched agents for labor ("Team") and is previewing a menu-pricing agent ([NRN, 2026-10-07](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/toast-unveils-an-array-of-new-ai-agents-for-restaurants)). Operator AI is moving from answering questions to taking action, and Toast IQ can already act, e.g. 86-ing items ([Toast newsroom](https://pos.toasttab.com/news/toast-iq-restaurant-trends-report-ai-assistant)). Margins leave no room for idle seats: full-service median pre-tax income is about 2.8% of sales ([Vanta Insights, citing NRA](https://vantainsights.com/insights/restaurant-profit-margins)).

**Boldest version:** service runs on an agent that keeps simulating the rest of the night: turn times, seating, waitlist offers, holds released to the booking widget, when to text a late party. It acts on its own within limits the operator sets ("auto-offer freed tables to the waitlist; ask me before any reseat"). The tape chart stops being a record and becomes the agent's plan, which the human edits.

**First wedge:** an "Autopilot" side panel on `apps/hospitality/src/pages/TimelinePage.tsx`. It subscribes to the existing SSE stream (`services/reservations/src/services/sse-connection-manager.ts`) and reads the waitlist (`waitlist.ts`) and table state (`table-state-machine.ts`). It proposes moves that render as ghost blocks on Rialto's `TapeChart` (`packages/rialto/src/components/TapeChart`), and one tap approves a move through the existing reservation and waitlist routes. The proposals are generated as catalog-constrained UI by the agent service, which shows off generative UI in a context where it earns its keep.

**What would kill it:** trust. One bad suggestion during peak service and the host never looks at the panel again. The suggestion quality needs to be measured (an eval harness replaying recorded nights) before anything acts on its own.

**Charter fit:** theme 2 (operator daily workflow: "how is tonight going?") taken to its logical end.

**Scores:** ambition 4 · feasibility 4 · wow 5

### 3. Seat-Yield Engine

**Pitch:** stop selling tables and start selling seat-hours: fill the dead 5:30 slot and make the 8:00 rush pay for itself.

**Who hurts today:** a neighbourhood bistro is 100% full Friday 7-9 and 30% full Tuesday at 5:30, and charges the same for both. Its book shows demand clearly, but the only levers it has are "accept" and "decline".

**Why now:** 42% of operators said they were unprofitable in 2025, up from 29% ([NRA State of the Industry 2026, via WTOP](https://wtop.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/SOI-2026-Report-Watermarked.pdf)). 87% saw food costs rise in H1 2026 ([Restaurant365 mid-year](https://www.restaurant365.com/guides/2026-state-of-the-restaurant-industry-mid-year-report/)). Toast is previewing a menu-pricing agent ([NRN](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/toast-unveils-an-array-of-new-ai-agents-for-restaurants)). Square's own guidance says dynamic pricing works when it rewards off-peak behaviour rather than surging ([Square](https://squareup.com/us/en/townsquare/dynamic-pricing-for-restaurants)). Bob Bob Ricard ran 25% off-peak discounts as a real-world example ([Denver DiningOut](https://diningout.com/denver/sliding-scale/)).

**Boldest version:** every slot has a price signal. That includes deposit size, a prepaid tasting option, an off-peak perk ("free dessert at 5:30") and a minimum spend for the best tables. The engine learns elasticity across a group's venues and runs the policy itself. The owner reviews a weekly yield report that shows money gained, not just covers.

**First wedge:** a RevPASH heat map (venue × day × 15-minute slot), computed from historical reservations and seat counts (`Table.maxCovers` in `services/reservations/prisma/schema.prisma`). Add a what-if simulator: "if Tue 5:30 had a perk and Fri 8:00 a $25 deposit, here is the projected fill". Then wire slot-level deposit tiers into the existing cancellation-policy package (`packages/cancellation-policy`) and `deposit-venue.ts`. RevPASH is already an unchecked Phase 5 item in `docs/plans/2026-01-31-hospitality-platform-roadmap.md`.

**What would kill it:** guest backlash. "Surge pricing for dinner" is a headline that writes itself, as Wendy's learned (per [Square](https://squareup.com/us/en/townsquare/dynamic-pricing-for-restaurants)). The engine has to frame everything as incentives and guarantees, never as surges.

**Charter fit:** theme 2, and sharpens the deposits theme (reliability of the deposit state machine).

**Scores:** ambition 4 · feasibility 4 · wow 4

### 4. Guest Passport

**Pitch:** your best guests carry their good reputation to every venue in the network, and the platform giants don't own it.

**Who hurts today:** a guest with 30 flawless visits at one independent place books a sister venue across town and is treated as a stranger. They're asked for a deposit, get no recognition and are seated at the worst table. Meanwhile the platform the venue books through is mining that relationship for its marketplace.

**Why now:** DashOS ties SevenRooms guest tools to "blinded and aggregated insights from the DoorDash Marketplace" ([Technobezz, 2026-09-30](https://www.technobezz.com/news/doordash-launches-dashos-restaurant-platform)). American Express now owns both Resy and Tock on a shared platform ([SevenRooms comparison](https://sevenrooms.com/blog/restaurant-reservation-system-comparison-guide/)). The Boston Globe framed the reservation-platform fight as one where "the real prize could be your data" ([Boston Globe, 2026-08-17](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/08/17/lifestyle/reservation-platforms-sevenrooms-opentable-resy/)). Guest data is consolidating into three walled gardens.

**Boldest version:** a guest-held, consented credential ("reliable diner, 30 visits, 0 no-shows, nut allergy"). Any venue on the network can verify it, and guest agents (idea #1) present it automatically. Deposits waived and priority seating become network-wide perks. Independents get the same network effect as the giants, and the guest owns the data.

**First wedge:** recognition at the group level, with no cross-tenant sharing yet. A `VenueGroup` (already in the schema) shares a consented guest graph across its venues. `guest-recognition.ts` and `guest-reliability.ts` resolve across sister venues, and the booking widget greets "Welcome back, Sam" with deposit waived on reliability. Add a guest-facing "my passport" page reached from the existing manage-reservation link (`apps/hospitality/src/pages/ManageReservationPage.tsx`).

**What would kill it:** consent and privacy law (sharing guest data across venues needs explicit opt-in), plus the cold-start problem every network faces. The group-level wedge avoids both, but it isn't the big idea until it crosses owners.

**Charter fit:** fits the target user (owner-operator of 1-10 venues). The full version touches the "no auth-model changes" guardrail for guest identity, which needs human sign-off. That's right for anything identity-shaped.

**Scores:** ambition 5 · feasibility 3 · wow 4

### 5. Kitchen-Aware Booking

**Pitch:** take bookings the kitchen can actually cook, and know tomorrow's prep before the doors open.

**Who hurts today:** the book allows 40 covers between 7:00 and 7:30 because the tables are free. The grill station can handle 25. Tickets back up, tables turn late, and the 8:30s wait at the bar. Separately, the chef preps from gut feel and throws out a hotel pan of short rib on a slow Wednesday.

**Why now:** 87% of operators saw food cost increases in H1 2026 ([Restaurant365](https://www.restaurant365.com/guides/2026-state-of-the-restaurant-industry-mid-year-report/)). POS-only forecasting tools report 15-25% waste reductions ([Fast.io roundup, vendor-reported](https://www.fast.io/resources/best-ai-for-restaurants-2026.md)), and Metafoodx raised $9.4M on production forecasting ([Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/metafoodx-9-4-million-raised-for-fighting-food-waste)). Reservations are the best forward-demand signal a kitchen has, and almost nobody connects them to prep.

**Boldest version:** front of house and back of house share one model of the night. Pacing caps are set by station capacity, not seat count. Guests can pre-select or pre-pay dishes at booking. Prep lists, purchase orders and 86 predictions come from the forward book plus dietary data, and the kitchen pushes back on the booking engine in real time.

**First wedge:** first, a "tomorrow's prep sheet" built from booked covers, party sizes, occasions and `Guest.dietaryRestrictions` (schema already has it; surfaced in `briefing.ts`). Second, a cover-pacing rule per 15-minute window, enforced in `slot-rules.ts` (which already carries `pacingRules` and `durationRules`). Third, an optional "pre-order a dish" step in the booking widget. No POS required.

**What would kill it:** without POS ticket data the forecast is crude (covers, not dishes). The full vision needs exactly the POS integration the charter rules out.

**Charter fit:** challenges the "POS integration" non-goal. Argument for moving it: integration is the only way the kitchen half becomes real. The wedge deliberately stays POS-free and uses pre-orders as a substitute signal, so the non-goal only needs to move if the wedge proves demand.

**Scores:** ambition 4 · feasibility 3 · wow 3

## Wildcard: The Self-Driving Restaurant

**Pitch:** point the same autonomous loop that builds this repo at the restaurant itself, and let the owner act as the code reviewer.

**Who hurts today:** every operator. Duration rules, pacing, deposit thresholds and waitlist quotes are set once at onboarding and never revisited, because nobody has time to run experiments on a live business.

**Why now:** this repo already runs a closed loop of sensors, regressions, issues, agent PRs, review and merge (`.claude/skills/learning-loop/`, `.claude/skills/implement-queue/`, `packages/agent-core/src/feedback-loop.ts`). The operator-agent wave (Toast IQ, DashOS) shows the industry wants agents that act ([NRN](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/toast-unveils-an-array-of-new-ai-agents-for-restaurants)). Nobody is yet treating a venue's operating policy as code that improves itself.

**Boldest version (10-year):** every venue setting is versioned code. The venue's sensors (no-show rate, turn-time drift, waitlist abandonment, RevPASH) file issues against its own config. Agents propose a change ("lengthen 5-6-top duration to 115 min on Saturdays"), test it as an A/B on real nights, and open a "venue PR" with the measured result. The owner merges or rejects it from their phone. The restaurant gets measurably better every week without anyone running it as a science project. Taken to the extreme, staff can file "issues" in plain language ("table 14 is always drafty") and the loop handles them.

**First wedge:** venue settings history plus a single sensor, duration-rule drift. Compare actual seated-to-closed times to `DEFAULT_DURATION_RULES` in `slot-rules.ts`, and present a proposed diff to the owner with evidence. One click applies it.

**What would kill it:** in a restaurant every experiment runs on paying guests. The appetite for automated change in hospitality may be far lower than in software. There's also little behavioural data at Tier-1 scale.

**Charter fit:** the purest expression of "make the platform more convincingly self-improving", applied to the product instead of the repo.

**Scores:** ambition 5 · feasibility 2 · wow 5

## Pick of the week: Agent Front Door

Bet on **#1**. Timing is the main reason: three weeks ago the industry's answer to guest agents was a ban, and every piece a venue-side answer needs now exists (MCP, agent payment tokens, ChatGPT and Google booking). It has the most leverage of any idea here:

- It makes the hospitality demo feel like a product a venue would pay for: "AI assistants can book you safely, on your terms".
- It showcases the agent platform on both sides of the table.
- It reuses more existing code than anything else on the list. The availability, hold and deposit routes and the agent tool definitions are already written.

It is also the foundation for #2, #3 and #4: negotiation needs yield (#3) and reputation (#4), and every agent booking flows into Autopilot (#2).
