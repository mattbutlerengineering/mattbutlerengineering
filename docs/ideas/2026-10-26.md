## The five

### 1. Switching Engine

**Pitch:** Send us your Resy or Tock export tonight; tomorrow morning every guest, note, allergy and future booking is here and your book never went dark.

**Who hurts today, and how.** A two-venue owner in Napa runs a winery tasting room on Tock and a bistro on Resy. Amex is folding Tock into Resy, and they've been told to expect the change sometime between this summer and 2027. They'd rather leave both, but 6,000 guest records, 340 future bookings and three years of staff notes are trapped in two systems. Switching means a weekend of CSV wrangling and a week of double-booked tables. So they stay, and keep paying.

**Why now.** Tock's consumer app and site are being decommissioned and its venues moved under Resy **(older, Feb 2026)** ([Frequent Miler](https://frequentmiler.com/after-purchasing-tock-amex-to-fold-it-into-resy-in-summer-2026/), [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91496951/amex-resy-tock-restaurant-reservation-wars)): thousands of venues face a forced migration either way. DoorDash's DashOS (2026-09-30) is a bet on deeper lock-in, binding reservations to marketplace and loyalty data ([QSR](https://www.qsrmagazine.com/story/inside-doordashs-push-to-connect-every-restaurant-channel/)). The opposite bet — leaving is cheap — has no owner. LLMs can now map a messy export to a schema in minutes, which used to be a services engagement.

**Boldest version.** Switching costs go to zero. An agent reads any incumbent's export (or, with the owner's credentials, their admin screens), rebuilds the guest book with identity resolution, re-creates every future booking against the venue's real floor plan, translates the old system's shift and pacing rules, and runs both systems in parallel for a week, flagging any divergence. The migration report is the sales pitch: "here's what we found in your data that your old system never showed you."

**First wedge (2-4 weeks).** A "Bring your book" step in venue onboarding that accepts a Resy/Tock/SevenRooms CSV, has an agent propose a column mapping, previews merges, then imports:

- Guests through the existing pure merge helpers in `services/reservations/src/services/guest-identity.ts` (`applyDietaryRestrictions`, `buildGuestUpdateData`) and the `@@unique([venueId, email])` / `([venueId, phone])` constraints on `Guest` in `services/reservations/prisma/schema.prisma`.
- Future bookings through `services/reservations/src/services/book-slot.ts`, with conflicts reported, not forced, using the stranded-reservation check in `availability-config-checks.ts`.
- Hours through the same shape as `packages/availability/src/legacy-migration.ts`, which already turns "one set of hours per weekday" into schedules and validates before returning — the pattern an importer needs.
- Onboarding UI under `apps/hospitality/src/components/venue-onboarding/`.

Demo: drop a synthetic 500-guest Tock export, watch the agent map it, see the merged guest book and the three bookings it refused to place.

**What would kill it.** Incumbent terms of service may forbid exporting guest data, or exports may strip notes and allergies. And guest consent: guests agreed to a privacy policy with Resy, not with us. Without a lawful-basis answer per region, the import is a liability.

**Charter fit.** Theme (3), onboarding → first booking, taken to its logical end: the fastest first booking is one the venue already had. Also showcases the agent platform doing real data work.

**Scores.** Ambition 4 · Feasibility 4 · Wow 4

### 2. Menu P&L

**Pitch:** Your menu knows what every dish costs you, tells you which ones lose money tonight, and drafts the fix — at one honest price for every guest.

**Who hurts today, and how.** A barbecue owner sees brisket cost jump and has two bad options: raise prices and lose regulars, or pull the dish. They pick the second blind, without knowing that the beef ribs were already their lowest-margin plate and that the pulled-pork platter with two sides would hold margin and fill the gap. That knowledge lives in a spreadsheet nobody has opened since opening.

**Why now.** Restaurants are pulling brisket and ribs rather than reprice ([Atlanta News First, 2026-09-22](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2026/09/22/some-barbecue-restaurants-are-pulling-brisket-ribs-their-menus-due-high-beef-prices/)); 87% of operators reported food cost increases in H1 2026 **(older)** ([Restaurant365](https://www.restaurant365.com/guides/2026-state-of-the-restaurant-industry-mid-year-report/)). Meanwhile states are legislating against per-person pricing — Maryland's ban took effect 2026-10-01 ([Skadden](https://skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/05/maryland-becomes-the-first-state-to-restrict-surveillance-pricing)), with Colorado and Connecticut following ([Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2026/05/maryland-leads-wave-of-state-surveillance-pricing)). So the margin lever has to be menu design, not personalised prices. And guests are punishing fake menus: AI-generated food photos drew a backlash in September ([NBC](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/tech/ai-food-images-jump-from-online-to-irl-menus/6542890/)).

**Boldest version.** The menu is a live financial instrument. Each dish carries its recipe cost (from invoice photos the agent reads), its contribution margin, its sell-through from the book and its real photo from the pass. Every Monday the agent proposes a menu: retire this, rename that, move this to the top, swap this cut. Prices change only on the menu, for everyone, with a signed log proving no guest ever saw a different price. Sold-out items vanish from every channel the second the kitchen taps "86".

**First wedge (2-4 weeks).** An additive `costCents` column on `MenuItem` (today it has only `priceCents` and `status`, `services/reservations/prisma/schema.prisma`), a margin column in the menu editor (`apps/hospitality/src/components/menus/`, `apps/hospitality/src/pages/MenusPage.tsx`), and a "what if beef is +30%?" question in the venue chat (`apps/hospitality/src/pages/ChatPage.tsx`, which already streams catalog-rendered answers via `@mbe/rialto-catalog`) that answers with a generated table of affected dishes and proposed changes. Applying a proposal goes through `services/reservations/src/routes/menus.ts`; the public menu (`public-menus.ts`) shows the result. No invoice OCR in the wedge — costs are typed in.

**What would kill it.** Data entry. Recipe costing is tedious, and owners who won't maintain a spreadsheet won't maintain this either, unless invoice reading actually works. Without a POS, sell-through is inferred from pre-orders, not real sales.

**Charter fit.** Fits hospitality theme (2), "how is tonight going?", extended to "is tonight making money?". Brushes the POS non-goal: the wedge needs no POS, but the boldest version wants sales data. Argue the non-goal moves to "no POS _replacement_", allowing a read-only sales import.

**Scores.** Ambition 4 · Feasibility 4 · Wow 4

### 3. Lost Demand Ledger

**Pitch:** See every guest you turned away — the 7:30 Saturday searches that found nothing — and what it would earn to open the patio or add a second seating.

**Who hurts today, and how.** A neighbourhood bistro is "full" every Friday and Saturday and assumes that's the ceiling. It's not: 140 parties a week searched the widget for a time that wasn't there and left. The owner never sees them, so they never test a 5:15 seating, a bar-only booking type or a second venue across town. Their reservation system only counts the bookings it made.

**Why now.** Full-service traffic is falling: the NRA has recorded net traffic declines in 16 of 17 months, and Black Box estimates 9% of full-service restaurants could close in 2026 **(older)** ([Dallas Express](https://dallasexpress.com/?p=333147), [FSR](https://www.fsrmagazine.com/feature/nine-percent-of-full-service-restaurants-at-risk-of-closing-in-2026-black-box-warns)). In a shrinking market, unmet demand is the scarcest signal an owner has. And discovery is moving to agents — Yelp Assistant now books OpenTable directly ([Yelp](https://blog.yelp.com/news/fall-product-release-2026/)) — whose failed queries leave no trace at the venue unless the venue records them.

**Boldest version.** Every channel — widget, agents, phone bot, walk-in turned away at the door — writes to one ledger of asked-for-but-not-sold seat-hours. The platform turns it into decisions: "a 5:15 seating would have sold 22 covers last month", "your Saturday turn-down rate is 3x your Tuesday's, raise the Saturday deposit", and for groups, "your overflow concentrates in this postcode; that's where venue #3 goes". Lost demand becomes the input for hours, pricing, staffing and site selection.

**First wedge (2-4 weeks).** Count, without PII, each public availability search that returned no slot at the requested time, in `services/reservations/src/routes/public-availability.ts`, keyed by venue, date, requested minute and party size. Aggregate it alongside the counts-only funnel in `services/reservations/src/services/booking-metrics.ts` (which already promises no PII in its shape). Show a "turned-away heatmap" on the dashboard (`apps/hospitality/src/components/dashboard/`) and one what-if: "add a seating at X" previewed through the existing `availability-preview.ts` engine to estimate how many of those searches would have booked.

**What would kill it.** Noise: one guest searching six times looks like six lost parties, and bots inflate everything. Without de-duplication by session (the widget's `originSessionId` concept helps) the numbers lie, and an owner who acts on a lie stops trusting the product.

**Charter fit.** Fits theme (2) operator daily workflow and the platform theme (2) "observability that feeds the sensor loop" — the venue gets the same sensor-driven treatment the repo gives itself.

**Scores.** Ambition 3 · Feasibility 5 · Wow 4

### 4. Wait Truth

**Pitch:** Publish your real walk-in wait, live, so Yelp's robot and every guest's assistant read it instead of calling your host stand mid-rush.

**Who hurts today, and how.** A no-reservations ramen bar's host is seating a six-top while the phone rings for the fourth time in ten minutes: "how long is the wait for two?" Some callers are now bots. Each call costs a minute of the most expensive minute of the night, and the answer the host gives ("about 30?") is a guess.

**Why now.** Yelp's "Check for Me" (October 2026) has an AI phone walk-in restaurants for the wait and text it back, and coverage notes Yelp hasn't said what happens when the restaurant's own AI answers ([Android Headlines](https://www.androidheadlines.com/2026/10/yelp-ai-agent-wait-times-restaurant-reservations.html), [Yelp](https://blog.yelp.com/news/fall-product-release-2026/)). Bot-to-bot phone calls are the worst possible protocol. Resy banning agent accounts ([Restaurant Business](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/technology/ai-agents-can-help-diners-book-table-it-can-also-get-them-banned)) shows incumbents treating agents as threats; a venue that _publishes_ for agents gets the traffic.

**What's new vs. 2026-10-11 #1 (Agent Front Door).** That idea was a negotiation protocol for _reservations_. This one is for _walk-ins_, the half of the industry with no booking to negotiate, and it's read-only: a public, cacheable truth feed, not a transaction. It needs no agent cooperation to ship, and it removes phone load on day one.

**Boldest version.** Every venue on the platform publishes a signed, live "state of the door" — current wait by party size, bar seats open, kitchen closing in 40 minutes, patio closed for rain — that any agent, map or phone bot can read. Guests join the waitlist from wherever they found it, and the venue's phone line answers wait questions itself from the same truth. Waits become accurate because they're computed from the floor plan, not guessed.

**First wedge (2-4 weeks).** A public, rate-limited, cache-friendly `GET /public/:slug/wait` built on `services/reservations/src/services/waitlist-estimate.ts` (which already estimates from table state and occupying reservations) and the public scoping in `public-venue-scope.ts`, returning wait by party size with a generated-at timestamp. Embed it on the booking widget (`apps/hospitality/src/components/booking-widget/`) with a "join the waitlist" button wired to `public-waitlist.ts`, and publish it as JSON-LD plus an `llms.txt`-style description so agents can find it. Demo: a scripted "guest agent" asks for a two-top and joins the line without a call.

**What would kill it.** Accuracy. A published wait that's wrong is worse than a guessed one, because guests turn up angry with a screenshot. Also distribution: Yelp has to choose to read the feed instead of calling.

**Charter fit.** Fits theme (1), close the guest-facing loop, for walk-ins; showcases the agent platform from the venue side.

**Scores.** Ambition 3 · Feasibility 5 · Wow 3

### 5. Crew from Covers

**Pitch:** Tomorrow's bookings write tomorrow's crew plan — how many servers, which hours — and offer the open shifts to your staff before you've finished your coffee.

**Who hurts today, and how.** A GM builds next week's schedule on Thursday from habit: four servers Friday, three Tuesday. Then a 20-top books Tuesday and a Friday wedding party cancels. Tuesday is understaffed and burns out the crew; Friday is overstaffed and burns margin. Labor is the line that decides profit: unprofitable full-service operators run labor near 42.9% of sales against 34.2% for profitable ones **(older)** ([Duck Hub, citing NRA](https://www.duck-hub.com/blog/restaurant-profit-margin-statistics)).

**Why now.** The forward book is the best labor forecast a venue has, and this repo already has it hour by hour. Reservation systems are being absorbed into larger stacks (DashOS, Resy+Toast) that couple the book to ordering and loyalty, not to labor ([QSR](https://www.qsrmagazine.com/story/inside-doordashs-push-to-connect-every-restaurant-channel/), [Expedite](https://www.expedite.news/p/toast-resy-and-tock-and-amex)). Nobody has closed the book → crew loop for independents.

**What's new vs. 2026-10-11 seed #8.** That seed was dropped for challenging the non-goal head-on. This version argues the non-goal should move, and narrows the scope: a _crew plan and shift offers_, never payroll, timekeeping or wage calculation.

**Boldest version.** Labor flexes with demand the way the book does. Each booking changes the crew curve in real time; when a 20-top lands, the platform offers the extra shift to the right people in seconds and tells the owner what it costs. Covers per labor hour becomes the number owners watch, next to RevPASH.

**First wedge (2-4 weeks).** A read-only "covers by hour → staff needed" curve for the next 7 days, from reservations plus a per-venue ratio (e.g. 1 server per 16 covers), shown on the timeline (`apps/hospitality/src/components/timeline/`). It uses the resolved service hours from `packages/availability/src/resolve-day.ts` and the team list from `services/reservations/src/services/venue-team.ts`. No shift records, no new paid service; "offer this shift" is a stub that drafts the message.

**What would kill it.** It's the payroll/staffing non-goal: scheduling software is a crowded, low-price category, and the moment you hold shifts you inherit predictive-scheduling laws and wage disputes. Staff trust is fragile too — a tool that cuts shifts automatically is a tool staff hate.

**Charter fit.** **Challenges** the hospitality non-goal "payroll/staffing". It should move to "no payroll", because labor is the line between profit and loss, the wedge is read-only demand data the repo already owns, and nothing else in the charter touches the cost that most decides whether a venue survives.

**Scores.** Ambition 4 · Feasibility 3 · Wow 3

## Wildcard: Covers-Backed Buying Co-op

**Pitch:** A hundred independent restaurants pool their booked covers and buy next month's beef together, at chain prices.

**Who hurts today, and how.** A single steakhouse buys beef at spot price from one distributor, with no leverage and no forecast. A 500-unit chain hedges, contracts forward and pays less for the same box. The independent pulls brisket off the menu ([Atlanta News First, 2026-09-22](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2026/09/22/some-barbecue-restaurants-are-pulling-brisket-ribs-their-menus-due-high-beef-prices/)); the chain doesn't.

**Why now.** Consolidation (Uber + ezCater, DoorDash + SevenRooms, Amex + Tock) concentrates demand data in platforms that don't buy food. A reservations platform holds the one asset that makes forward buying possible: covers already promised, by date, by party size, with dietary data.

**Boldest version (10-year).** The booking platform becomes a purchasing co-op. Booked covers × menu mix (Menu P&L, #2) = a forward demand curve for each ingredient across every member venue. The co-op contracts forward with farms and distributors, and members' deposits and prepaid experiences become the collateral. Independents get chain economics without becoming a chain, and the co-op's demand data — owned by its members — is a better crop forecast than anyone else's.

**First wedge.** A thought experiment, not a sprint: a script over seed data that multiplies booked covers by menu mix to print a per-ingredient forward demand for one venue for 14 days. It needs Menu P&L's cost data to mean anything.

**What would kill it.** Everything: commodities regulation, co-op governance, distributor retaliation, the cash to front a forward contract, and the fact that a reservation is a soft promise. A software company running a food co-op is a different company.

**Charter fit.** Challenges the "POS integration" and "new paid infrastructure" guardrails and the whole "small venue SaaS" frame. It's here to stretch the horizon.

**Scores.** Ambition 5 · Feasibility 1 · Wow 5

## Pick of the week: Switching Engine

The other four make the product better once a venue is on it. Switching Engine is the only one that gets a venue on it. Adoption, not features, is what stops an owner paying for a new reservations system, and the market is handing over a forcing event: thousands of Tock venues will migrate whether they like it or not, while DoorDash bets the other way on lock-in. It's also the best showcase for the agent platform in this list: an agent doing messy, high-stakes data work (schema mapping, identity merges, conflict-aware re-booking) with a reviewable preview before anything is written — the same propose-then-apply discipline the repo uses on itself. And the wedge builds almost entirely on code that already exists (`guest-identity.ts`, `book-slot.ts`, the `legacy-migration.ts` pattern, venue onboarding). Runner-up: Lost Demand Ledger, which is the cheapest to ship and would make the dashboard tell the owner something no incumbent does.
