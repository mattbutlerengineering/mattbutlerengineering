# Big ideas — 2026-10-12

Second entry. It runs one day after the first one (`2026-10-11.md`), so the market context from yesterday's search still holds: guest-side agents went mainstream, Resy banned one, DoorDash launched DashOS, and Toast launched labor agents. This week deliberately avoids all six of yesterday's picks (Agent Front Door, Tonight Autopilot, Seat-Yield Engine, Guest Passport, Kitchen-Aware Booking, Self-Driving Restaurant). One idea evolves a seed that yesterday dropped (#13, private dining), and one supplies something a top-5 idea from yesterday was missing (the eval harness that idea #2 said it needed). Both say which entry they extend.

**About the "last 30 days" brief:** search turned up few hospitality-tech items dated 2026-09-12 to 2026-10-12. Each claim below carries its date. Anything older than the window is marked **(older)** and is used as background, not as the "why now".

**The market in one line, this week:** the money is going to platforms that do everything for independents. Owner raised $240M at a $2.3B valuation ([SiliconANGLE, 2026-08-28](https://siliconangle.com/2026/08/28/owner-raises-240m-for-its-restaurant-management-platform/)) and Chowbus raised $81M ([Restaurant Technology News, 2026-03](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2026/03/chowbus-raises-81-million-to-expand-its-ai-powered-platform-for-independent-restaurants/)) **(older)**. Meanwhile the independents themselves are shrinking: their count fell 2.3% in 2025, to 412,498 locations ([NRN](https://www.nrn.com/independent-restaurants/the-independent-restaurant-sector-shrunk-by-2-3-in-2025)) **(older)**, and the way guests dine is changing (more solo diners, smaller orders). Most ideas below are about earning money from the shapes of demand that today's table-centric software can't see.

**Repo facts this entry relies on (files opened this run):**

- The `Table` model is the unit of inventory: `capacity`, `minCovers`, `maxCovers`, `priority` (`services/reservations/prisma/schema.prisma`). There's no seat, menu or event model. `Guest` is scoped per venue (`@@unique([venueId, email])`).
- Availability matches a party to one table (`findBestTable`, `findSuitableTables` in `services/reservations/src/services/availability.ts`). Durations and pacing are per venue (`durationRules` and `pacingRules` in `slot-rules.ts`).
- The live event vocabulary is `reservation:created|updated|cancelled`, `hold:created|released|confirmed` and `table:updated` (`services/reservations/src/services/events.ts`), streamed by `routes/events.ts`. Nothing persists the stream. "Event sourcing" is still unchecked in Phase 5 of `docs/plans/2026-01-31-hospitality-platform-roadmap.md`.
- Offline today is read-only. `apps/hospitality/src/hooks/useReservations.ts` falls back to cached reservations (`isFromCache`, `lastSyncedAt`), and `components/OfflineBanner.tsx` warns the host. The roadmap's PWA track leaves IndexedDB, delta sync, the offline mutation queue and LWW conflict resolution unchecked.
- Generative UI runs through the agent service (`services/agent/src/routes/gen-ui.ts`, built on `@mbe/rialto-catalog`) and renders in `apps/gen/src/components/SpecRenderer.tsx`.
- Post-visit email exists (`services/reservations/src/services/post-visit-notifier.ts`, with HMAC-signed guest tokens). The `remembered-on-arrival` idea brief measured zero rating, survey or feedback capture anywhere in the product (`docs/features/remembered-on-arrival/idea.md`).

## The five

### 1. Event Desk

_Extends 2026-10-11 seed #13 (private dining and buyout marketplace). Yesterday dropped it as "a strong candidate for a later week". What's new: it's the whole events funnel (catering, group dinners, buyouts) with an agent drafting the proposal, rather than a marketplace._

**Pitch:** a party of 40 emails at 11pm, and before you're up they have a priced menu proposal, a deposit link and the back room on hold.

**Who hurts today:** a 70-seat neighbourhood restaurant gets three private-event inquiries a week, by email, Instagram DM and phone. The owner answers them between lunch and dinner service, two days late, with a PDF from 2023. One in three goes to the place that answered first. When an event does book, it lives in a notebook, so the reservation system still sells the back room's tables to the public on the same night.

**Why now:**

- Catering is outgrowing restaurants: about 5.3% a year versus about 3%. Top performers grew it 10-20% year on year in Q1 2026, and 60% of catering orders come from 5% of customers ([Food On Demand, 2026-07-15](https://foodondemand.com/07152026/catering-outpaces-restaurant-industry-growth/); vendor data from Monkey). **(older)**
- Hotels are handing F&B to specialist operators, so restaurants increasingly sell into events they don't host ([Hotel Management, 2026-09](https://www.hotelmanagement-network.com/features/when-hotels-outsource-dining/)).
- The event-software incumbents are tiny and old (Perfect Venue's last round was a 2022 seed, [vcbacked](https://www.vcbacked.co/company/perfect-venue)) **(older)**, and none of them owns the table inventory.

**Boldest version:** events become the venue's highest-margin channel, run almost entirely by agents. The inquiry agent qualifies the lead, quotes from the venue's real availability and minimum-spend rules, and renders a proposal the guest can edit (menu tier, start time, headcount). It collects a scaled deposit and blocks the right tables on the floor plan. Then it runs the long tail: RSVP collection for group dinners, headcount changes, dietary roll-ups, final invoice. A buyout and a 10-top birthday are the same object at different sizes. Repeat corporate clients (the 5%) get a portal and standing orders.

**First wedge (2-4 weeks):**

- An `EventInquiry` model, added by an additive migration only.
- An agent route that turns free text into a structured request, alongside the existing `services/agent/src/routes/gen-agent-tools.ts`. It reuses `check_availability`.
- A proposal page rendered from a catalog spec, through `services/agent/src/routes/gen-ui.ts` and `apps/gen/src/components/SpecRenderer.tsx`.
- Confirming places multi-table holds through `services/reservations/src/services/hold.ts` and takes a deposit through `deposit-state-machine.ts`.

Demo: paste an email and get a live proposal, then the event's block appears on the Timeline.

**What would kill it:** events are high-touch and relationship-driven. An owner may not trust an agent to quote a $6,000 buyout, and a single wrong quote costs real money. The proposal has to stay a draft the owner approves until it has earned trust.

**Charter fit:** theme 1 (guest-facing loop) on a new, larger booking type, and the generative UI earns its keep in a customer-facing document. No non-goal is challenged.

**Scores:** ambition 4 · feasibility 4 · wow 5

### 2. Service Never Stops

**Pitch:** when the internet or your software vendor goes down on a Saturday, your host stand doesn't notice.

**Who hurts today:** at 7:30 on a Saturday the venue's Wi-Fi drops, or the vendor has an outage. The tablet shows yesterday's cached book, or a spinner. The host goes back to a clipboard, double-seats table 12, loses the waitlist, and spends Sunday morning reconciling no-shows by hand. In this repo it's the same story: the Timeline falls back to read-only cached data (`apps/hospitality/src/hooks/useReservations.ts`, `isFromCache`), and every write fails until the connection returns.

**Why now:**

- Toast had a 1.5-hour Online and Third-Party Ordering outage on 2026-04-28, plus a 51-minute ToastWeb outage that month ([isdown](https://isdown.app/status/toast/outage-history)) **(older)**.
- Toss POS in Korea failed twice in one day, at both lunch and dinner peaks ([Herald, 2026-06-26](https://mbiz.heraldcorp.com/article/10789922)) **(older)**.
- Toast's own offline guidance (updated 2026-06-30) says its POS keeps taking card payments offline, but online channels stop ([Toast support](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Outages-and-Disruptions-Toast-Online-Ordering)) **(older)**.

POS vendors made payments survive outages, and nobody did the same for the reservation book. Local-first sync (CRDTs, IndexedDB and service workers) is now mature enough for one engineer to ship.

**Boldest version:** the venue's iPad is the source of truth for tonight, and the cloud is just a peer. Every tablet in the room syncs peer to peer on the LAN. Seating, waitlist, walk-ins and status changes keep working with zero connectivity. The booking widget degrades gracefully: it closes online slots and leaves the room unaffected. When the link returns, the venue merges with a conflict report a human can read ("2 online bookings landed on tables you seated offline; here are the fixes"). Eventually the agents (yesterday's Autopilot) also run at the edge, so service intelligence survives the outage too. "Never stops" becomes a marketed guarantee.

**First wedge (2-4 weeks):** finish the roadmap's unchecked PWA track:

- Cache today's book and the active floor plan in IndexedDB.
- An offline mutation queue for seat, status and walk-in actions, through the same calls `TimelinePage.tsx` already makes.
- Last-writer-wins conflicts with a reconciliation screen.
- Turn `OfflineBanner.tsx` from a warning into a "working offline, 6 changes queued" status.

Demo: open DevTools, set the network to offline, seat four parties and add a walk-in, go back online, and watch it all reconcile against an online booking made in the meantime.

**What would kill it:** conflict semantics. Two hosts and the internet each make one decision about the same table, and any automatic merge rule will eventually seat two parties at it. The reconciliation UX is the whole product, and it's hard to test without real chaos.

**Charter fit:** theme 2 (operator daily workflow) and the services theme (reliability of the booking state machine), plus visible engineering craft. It runs on existing Cloudflare and PWA infrastructure. No non-goal is challenged; "native mobile apps" stays out because this is the PWA.

**Scores:** ambition 4 · feasibility 4 · wow 5

### 3. Sell the Seat

**Pitch:** stop turning away the solo diner and the "just a small plate" couple: sell your counter, bar and communal seats one at a time, for the time people actually stay.

**Who hurts today:** a solo guest wants 6:15 on a Thursday. The booking engine only knows tables, so it offers a 2-top for 90 minutes, which wastes a seat and blocks the 7:45. Or it shows "no availability" while three bar stools sit empty. Across town a couple on GLP-1s want two small plates and a glass of wine in 45 minutes, but every slot is sold as a full dinner.

**Why now:**

- Solo reservations rose 23% year on year, and solo diners spend $94 on average against $60 per head for everyone ([Axios, 2026-07-17](https://axios.com/2026/07/17/solo-dining-reservations-jump-opentable), OpenTable data) **(older)**. Toast puts solos under 1% of booked reservations, so the base is small but growing fast.
- 62% of GLP-1 users order less when they eat out, and half-portion options are the top menu feature that would influence their choice of restaurant ([Toast survey, 2026-08](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/data/glp-1-restaurant-habits)) **(older)**.
- Restaurant spending by GLP-1 users is now only about 4% below pre-medication levels, so this is "a behavioural shift, not a demand collapse" ([William Blair](https://www.williamblair.com/Insights/GLP1s-and-Restaurants-A-Behavioral-Shift-Not-a-Demand-Collapse)).

Demand is getting smaller, shorter and more solo, and table-based inventory can't express it.

**Boldest version:** inventory is a grid of seats × minutes, not tables × slots. Tables become just one way of grouping seats. The engine sells a single counter seat, a shared high-top seat, a 40-minute "aperitivo" visit, or a pair of bar seats that turns twice in one table-slot. Duration and minimum spend follow the visit type, not just the party size. The guest booking for one gets a seat with a view of the kitchen and a counter-friendly menu. The operator sees seat utilisation instead of table occupancy. This differs from yesterday's Seat-Yield Engine, which priced existing slots: here the thing being sold changes.

**First wedge (2-4 weeks):**

- A seat-capable table type: an additive `Table.seatMode` (`whole | per-seat`) on bar and counter tables in `services/reservations/prisma/schema.prisma`.
- `findSuitableTables` and `findBestTable` in `availability.ts` let several parties share one per-seat table up to its `capacity`.
- A `visitType` (full, light, counter) that selects a duration from `durationRules` in `slot-rules.ts`.
- A "counter seat" or "quick visit" choice in the booking widget (`apps/hospitality/src/components/booking-widget/`).
- On the Timeline, multiple bookings stack within one counter row on Rialto's `TapeChart`.

**What would kill it:** operational reality. Hosts think in tables, and per-seat seating breaks conflict detection, the table state machine (`table-state-machine.ts`: a table can't be both "seated" and "available") and every report. That's a deep refactor wearing a feature costume. And solos are still under 1% of bookings at Toast.

**Charter fit:** theme 1 (booking flows that feel production-grade for every guest, including the party of one). Neutral on non-goals.

**Scores:** ambition 4 · feasibility 3 · wow 4

### 4. Service Flight Simulator

_Supplies the "eval harness replaying recorded nights" that 2026-10-11 idea #2 (Tonight Autopilot) named as its precondition. What's new: the replay is a product in its own right, for training humans first and agents second._

**Pitch:** your new host gets to work a Saturday rush ten times before their first real one.

**Who hurts today:** a new host starts on a Thursday. Training means shadowing for two shifts, then the Friday push, where they quote a 20-minute wait that turns into 50 and seat a VIP by the bathroom. Many don't make it to week three. The owner spends money training people who leave and still gets inconsistent service from those who stay.

**Why now:**

- 51.8% of QSR workers who found onboarding inefficient quit during onboarding or training ([QSR Web, AllianceHCM survey](https://www.qsrweb.com/news/over-half-of-fast-food-workers-quit-early-when-onboarding-fails-report-finds/)) **(older)**.
- Turnover estimates run from about 75% to over 130% a year, and replacing a front-of-house worker costs about $1,056 (7shifts, via [Stealth Agents](https://stealthagents.com/research/restaurant-industry-staffing-costs-2026)) **(older, aggregator)**.
- 26% of operators used AI tools as of February 2026 (NRA, [2026 Hiring and Staffing insight](https://go.restaurant.org/rs/078-ZLA-461/images/2026-Research-Insight_Hiring-and-Staffing.pdf?version=0)) **(older)**.
- The agent wave makes evaluation urgent: Toast's labor agents launched 2026-10-07 ([NRN](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/toast-unveils-an-array-of-new-ai-agents-for-restaurants)), and nobody publishes how their service agents perform on a real night.

**Boldest version:** every service is recorded as an event stream, then anonymised and replayable. A new host plays last Saturday at 4× speed: walk-ins arrive, a 6-top runs long, and a regular shows up 30 minutes late. Their decisions are scored against what actually happened and against an expert policy. The same simulator benchmarks agents. An agent's suggestions only go live once it beats the house's best host across 50 recorded nights. Across many venues, the corpus of nights becomes a moat. It's the hospitality version of a flight simulator, and of a SWE-bench for service.

**First wedge (2-4 weeks):**

- Persist the existing SSE vocabulary (`services/reservations/src/services/events.ts`) as an append-only `reservation_events` table, through an additive migration. This ticks the roadmap's "Event sourcing" item.
- A replay mode on `apps/hospitality/src/pages/TimelinePage.tsx` that feeds recorded events through the same SSE client at adjustable speed.
- One scenario ("the 7:30 crunch") with injected walk-ins. The trainee's seat and quote decisions are scored by a small agent route in `services/agent/`.

Demo: replay a seeded night, make three decisions, and get a score with the actual outcome alongside.

**What would kill it:** a sim is only as good as its model of guests. Turn times and walk-out behaviour inferred from a thin event log may teach the wrong lessons, and a Tier-1 venue doesn't generate enough nights for the agent benchmark to mean much. Trainees may also find it a toy.

**Charter fit:** challenges the **payroll/staffing** non-goal at its edge. This isn't scheduling or payroll, but it is a staff product. Argument for moving the line: training is the cheapest labor lever an operator has, and the simulator doubles as the platform's agent-evaluation backbone ("make the platform more convincingly self-improving"). The event log alone fits theme 2 and the observability theme today.

**Scores:** ambition 4 · feasibility 4 · wow 5

### 5. Proof-of-Visit

**Pitch:** reviews only from people who actually ate here, signed by their reservation, and readable by the AI assistants that now choose where people book.

**Who hurts today:** a 30-seat ramen bar gets five one-star reviews in one evening from accounts that never visited, followed by an email offering to "fix" them for $300. Separately, a guest's AI assistant choosing between two places for Friday summarises review sites it can't verify. The places with the best actual track record have no way to prove it.

**Why now:**

- The FTC's final rule bans fake and AI-generated reviews and lets the agency seek civil penalties ([Hunton](https://hunton.com/hunton-retail-law-resource/ftc-issues-final-rule-targeting-fake-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials-including-those-generated-by-artificial-intelligence-ai-and-online-bots), 2024) **(older)**. Restaurant review extortion ("pay up or get slammed with one-star reviews") is documented ([CBS](https://cbsnews.com/sacramento/news/fake-online-product-reviews-ftc-proposed-rule-fine)) **(older)**.
- The new part is the reader. Since August, ChatGPT books restaurants through OpenTable, Resy and Yelp, and Google AI Mode books in the UK (sources in `2026-10-11.md`). Agents need machine-verifiable reputation more than humans ever did.

**Boldest version:**

- Every completed reservation can produce exactly one signed review credential, tied to the visit date and party size and verifiable by anyone.
- Venues publish a feed of verified-visit reviews that guest agents prefer over unverifiable stars.
- Private feedback goes to the operator first, with a recovery window before anything is public; it's honest, not suppression, because the guest can always publish.
- Across the network, "verified diner" scores become a portable trust layer that small independents can lean on against aggregator rankings they don't control.

**First wedge (2-4 weeks):**

- Extend `services/reservations/src/services/post-visit-notifier.ts` (it already signs per-guest HMAC tokens) to issue a single-use, reservation-scoped feedback token.
- Add a public feedback page next to `apps/hospitality/src/pages/ManageReservationPage.tsx`, with an additive `VisitFeedback` table.
- Operator-side, add a feedback inbox with a sentiment flag through the agent service.
- Publish a signed `/venues/:slug/reviews.json` feed that any agent can verify.

This also closes the measured gap from `remembered-on-arrival` (no feedback capture anywhere).

**What would kill it:**

- Distribution: Google and Yelp own where reviews are read, and a verified feed nobody consumes is just a nice page.
- Regulatory optics: the FTC rule also bans suppressing honest negative reviews, so the private-first window must be designed carefully, or it becomes the exact violation it claims to fix.
- Today's post-visit sends depend on `REDIS_URL`, which isn't configured in production (per `remembered-on-arrival/idea.md`).

**Charter fit:** theme 1 (closes the post-visit end of the guest loop). No non-goal is challenged. Pairs with yesterday's Guest Passport, but it attests to the venue, not the guest.

**Scores:** ambition 4 · feasibility 4 · wow 4

## Wildcard: Dark Hours Exchange

**Pitch:** your dining room is dark on Mondays and Tuesdays. Rent it, fully booked and staffed, to a chef who wants to run a pop-up, and the software builds the whole pop-up in an afternoon.

**Who hurts today:** two groups at once. An owner pays rent on a room that earns nothing two days a week. A talented line cook who wants to test a concept can't afford a lease, and the 2021 ghost-kitchen boom showed that a kitchen without a room and guests doesn't work. With independents shrinking 2.3% in 2025, full-service independents fastest at 2.6% ([NRN](https://www.nrn.com/independent-restaurants/the-independent-restaurant-sector-shrunk-by-2-3-in-2025)) **(older)**, idle hours are a cost few can carry.

**Boldest version (10-year):** an exchange for restaurant capacity by the hour, a kind of Airbnb for dining rooms. A chef describes a concept: "Oaxacan tasting menu, 24 covers, two seatings, $85". The platform compiles a complete temporary venue on a host restaurant's dark day: booking page, floor plan derived from the host's, deposit policy, guest list seeded from the chef's following, and a revenue split settled automatically. Hosts earn yield on dead hours. Chefs get a proving ground with real guests and real data. The best pop-ups graduate to their own lease with a guest list already built. The venue becomes a time-shared platform, not a fixed business.

**First wedge:** "venue from a prompt". The onboarding flow (`apps/hospitality/src/pages/VenueOnboardingPage.tsx`, `docs/features/first-venue-self-serve/`) plus a gen-UI spec produces a pop-up `Venue` under the host's `VenueGroup` (already in the schema). It borrows the host's floor plan and sets operating hours for one date range. Demo: type a concept and get a bookable pop-up page in a minute.

**What would kill it:** the physical and legal world. Health permits, liquor licences, insurance, kitchen equipment liability and who cleans the walk-in are all hard problems that software can't solve. Trust between host and guest chef is also the hardest version of the trust problem in this whole document.

**Charter fit:** challenges the charter's framing of the target user as an "owner-operator of 1-10 venues". Here the operator might be a chef with zero venues. Argument for moving it: it shows the multi-venue model (`VenueGroup`) and generative onboarding doing something no incumbent offers.

**Scores:** ambition 5 · feasibility 2 · wow 5

## Pick of the week: Service Flight Simulator

Bet on **#4**. It has the most leverage of anything this week, for four reasons:

- **It's the missing foundation under yesterday's pick.** Every agent idea from 2026-10-11 (Agent Front Door negotiation, Tonight Autopilot) is untrustworthy until it can be scored on real nights. The simulator is that score, and building it first de-risks the whole agent roadmap.
- **It reads as a product a venue would pay for.** "Train new hosts on your own Saturday nights" is a concrete pitch with a turnover cost (about $1,000+ per front-of-house replacement) attached, and no incumbent in the search results offers it.
- **It showcases the agent platform honestly.** The repo already runs a sensor → eval → merge loop on itself, and pointing that rigour at service decisions is the clearest proof of "convincingly self-improving".
- **The wedge is cheap and charter-clean.** Persisting the event stream ticks an open roadmap item, and replay reuses `TimelinePage.tsx` and the existing SSE client.

Runner-up: **Service Never Stops**, because "pull the network cable mid-demo and keep seating" is the best five-minute demo on this list.
